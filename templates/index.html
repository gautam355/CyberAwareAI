Phishing Awareness — What Every Employee Should Know

Over 90% of cyberattacks start with a phishing email. Here's how to spot and avoid them.

Urgency is a Red Flag

Phishing emails always create a sense of panic — "Your account expires in 2 hours!", "Act NOW or lose access". Legitimate companies don't rush you like this. If you feel pressured, slow down.

Check the Link Before Clicking

Hover over any link to see the real URL. Attackers use tricks like rnicrosoft.com (r+n looks like m). Never click — go directly to the official website.

Verify Before You Trust Authority

Attackers pretend to be your CEO, IT team, or HR. If your "boss" emails asking for a wire transfer or gift cards — call them directly to verify. Emails can be faked.

Never Share Your Password

Real IT teams will NEVER ask for your password. If any email or website is asking for your credentials — it's a phishing attempt. Report it immediately.

Check the Sender's Email Address

The display name can say "Microsoft Support" but the actual address might be support@gmail.com. Always check the actual email address, not just the name shown.

When in Doubt, Pick Up the Phone

If you're unsure about an email — especially one about passwords, money, or urgent action — call the person directly. 2 minutes on the phone can save hours of damage control.
